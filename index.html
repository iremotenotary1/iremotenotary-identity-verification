Domestic signers

Preparing for identity verification in the United States

Domestic remote online notarization sessions may involve government-issued photo identification, credential analysis, and additional platform-approved identity proofing. Depending on the platform and session, that may include knowledge-based questions or other approved methods.

  • Have an unexpired, readable government-issued photo ID ready when required.
  • Use a device with a working camera and microphone and a stable internet connection.
  • Confirm that the name on your ID matches the document you intend to sign.
  • Review whether your document and receiving agency accept remote online notarization.

Acceptable ID types and verification steps depend on the platform, notary commission rules, and session requirements. iRemoteNotary cannot guarantee that every listed identification document will be accepted in every session.

International signers

Preparing from outside the United States

For eligible international signers, iRemoteNotary uses supported platforms that can provide biometric identity-verification options rather than relying on U.S.-based knowledge questions as the primary international workflow.

  • Confirm platform availability, document eligibility, and recipient acceptance before booking.
  • Prepare an acceptable, unexpired government-issued photo ID supported by the session platform.
  • Test your camera, microphone, browser, and internet connection in a well-lit, private location.
  • Review your unsigned document and signing instructions before the live session.

International verification requirements can differ from domestic sessions. Confirm your ID, location, and document requirements before the appointment.

Fraud and impersonation awareness

Why identity screening matters in remote online notarization

Remote online notarization depends on confirming that the person on camera is the signer named in the document. Credential analysis, biometric comparison where supported, live audiovisual communication, and notarial screening can help reduce identity and impersonation risks.

No online process eliminates all fraud risk. Signers should protect their identification documents, use trusted networks, and follow platform instructions carefully. Report suspicious contact or impersonation attempts through appropriate consumer-protection channels when needed.

Prepare before the session

Four practical checks

1

Use a valid photo ID

Have an accepted government-issued photo ID available and make sure it is readable and unexpired where required.

2

Review the name

Compare the name on the document with the name on your ID before the session begins.

3

Prepare your device

Use a reliable camera, microphone, browser, and internet connection in a well-lit location.

4

Keep documents unsigned

Unless instructed otherwise, wait to sign until you are connected with the notary.
